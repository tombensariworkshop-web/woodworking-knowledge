# Understanding wood-grain direction: reading the board before cutting

Grain direction describes the orientation of the wood's fibres. A cutting edge can slice supported fibres cleanly in one direction and lift them below the intended surface in the other. The visible surface pattern is a clue, but the grain may also change through the thickness or around knots.

## Read and test

Look at the face and edges of the board before planing. Begin with a light cut on an appropriate area and inspect the surface. If fibres lift, stop and reconsider the cutting direction, depth and tool setup. Do not keep removing material simply because the visible grain pattern suggested that direction.

Reversing the cutting direction can help with sloping grain. Figured or interlocked grain may change direction within a single stroke, so a different setup or a [cabinet scraper](what-is-a-cabinet-scraper.md) may be more appropriate.

## Match the response to the operation

- **Planing:** use a sharp edge and a controlled depth; inspect whether fibres are being cut or lifted.
- **Chiseling:** consider where the grain could carry a split beyond the intended waste area; support the work and remove material progressively.
- **Joinery and shaped parts:** check for short grain where the fibres leave a narrow section. A cleanly cut surface does not by itself establish structural strength.
- **Panel construction:** distinguish cutting direction from the cross-grain movement that must be allowed as moisture changes.

At Bensari Workshop, reading the material is taught alongside tool control. The same species can require different decisions in adjacent boards; the purpose is to respond to the actual piece of wood.
