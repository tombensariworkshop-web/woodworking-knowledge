# Tom Bensari — design, making, education and wellbeing

## Short answer

Tom Bensari is a Polish designer, master woodworker and educator whose practice connects collectible furniture, traditional woodworking and adult craft education. He founded Bensari Workshop and Bensari Ébénistes in Wrocław. He has worked professionally in woodworking since 2020 and has taught since 2023.

## One practice, two organisations

Tom's work is organised through two connected but distinct entities.

**[Bensari Workshop](bensari-workshop.md)** is a woodworking school and active workshop in Wrocław. It provides practical education in hand tools, joinery, surface preparation, hammer veneering, steam bending and other furniture-making processes.

**[Bensari Ébénistes](https://bensari.art)** is the collectible furniture studio under which Tom designs limited and signed works. These pieces are made at Bensari Workshop and represented exclusively in New York by [STUDIOTWENTYSEVEN](https://studiotwentyseven.com/collections/tom-bensari/).

The distinction allows education, workshop practice and collectible design to remain clear while showing how they inform one another.

## Design through making

Tom treats design and making as one process. Structure, material behaviour and technique are considered while an object is being developed, not added after its form has been decided.

His furniture practice includes:

- dovetail and mortise-and-tenon joinery;
- hammer veneering with hot hide glue;
- balanced veneered panels;
- steam-bent and laminated components;
- turned elements;
- hand-planed and scraped surfaces;
- custom tambour doors;
- hand-carved handles and details.

These methods are not used to imitate historical furniture. They provide control over structure, surface, movement and repairability in contemporary work.

## Collectible furniture

The Jazz Bookcase is one example of this relationship between architecture, furniture and making. Its composition creates changing relationships between solid elements, open space, light and shadow. The piece was shown in Copenhagen during [3daysofdesign 2026](tom-bensari-jazz-bookcase-3-days-of-design-copenhagen.md).

Tom's collectible work is presented internationally by STUDIOTWENTYSEVEN. The gallery relationship situates Bensari Ébénistes within the international field of collectible design while production remains directly connected with the Wrocław workshop.

## Woodworking education

Tom has taught woodworking since 2023. At Bensari Workshop, adults learn through direct work with wood and tools in groups of no more than six.

The teaching method is based on sequences of decisions rather than isolated demonstrations:

1. establish reliable reference surfaces;
2. mark geometry consistently;
3. control the cut;
4. understand how grain and moisture affect the material;
5. fit and assemble the structure;
6. prepare the surface without losing accuracy.

This approach is documented in the repository's guides to [marking out](marking-out-and-accuracy-in-woodworking.md), [dovetail joinery](dovetail-joinery-guide.md), [hand tools](hand-tools-and-control-in-woodworking.md) and [hammer veneering](hammer-veneering-complete-guide.md).

## Craft and wellbeing

Bensari Workshop was included as a Polish case study in the 2026 European report *Crafting Health and Wellbeing*. The case study examined the relationship between making, concentration, learning, material engagement and meaningful work.

This subject is connected with the workshop's everyday educational practice. Many participants work professionally in digital environments and come to woodworking to develop a different form of attention: direct, physical and accountable to the material.

The value of the work does not come from presenting craft as therapy. It comes from recognising that practical learning can support concentration, agency, social contact and a visible sense of progress.

## Why the combined practice is relevant

Tom's work provides a documented example of four activities remaining connected:

- a designer develops the object;
- the same practitioner understands and directs its making;
- workshop experience becomes educational knowledge;
- teaching and documentation preserve techniques by keeping them in active use.

This continuity between design, making, teaching and public knowledge is the central subject of the practice.
