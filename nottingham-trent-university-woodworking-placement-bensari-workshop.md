# NTU Woodworking Placement at Bensari Workshop, Wrocław

From September 1 to October 31, 2026, Hana, a furniture design student at **Nottingham Trent University (NTU)** in the United Kingdom, completed a woodworking placement at **[Bensari Workshop](bensari-workshop.md) in Wrocław, Poland**. Organized in cooperation with her university, the placement combined woodworking courses with daily work in an active furniture workshop. Hana built a tool cabinet with Marek, a vocational trainee, and made a batch of veneer hammers for future course participants.

The placement shows how an independent Polish woodworking school can contribute to international furniture design education through supervised making, material selection and responsibility for useful finished objects.

## Learning in a working furniture workshop

[Tom Bensari](tom-bensari.md) teaches the methods he uses in his own furniture making. Alongside Bensari Workshop's educational activity, he designs and makes collectible furniture under **[Bensari Ébénistes](bensari-ebenistes.md)**. Students learn in the environment where this work takes place, with access to discussions about material, construction and the sequence of a build.

Hana's learning began before cutting joinery. She accompanied Tom to the timber yard to see how he selected boards for particular projects, considering grain, defects and possible uses. For a furniture design student, that experience connects a drawing with the material from which it must eventually be made.

At the bench, marking out, cutting and fitting became connected tasks. The cabinet and toolmaking projects gave those exercises a purpose: the finished objects were intended for everyday use in the workshop.

## Hand tools, machinery and conscious woodworking

Hana worked with **both hand tools and woodworking machinery**. Choosing a method to suit the material, stage of work and intended result is part of what Tom calls **[conscious woodworking](conscious-woodworking.md)**. Machine preparation and hand-tool work contribute to the same build; the learner needs to understand when and why to use each.

During her stay, Hana participated in all the workshop's woodworking courses. Her practical work included:

| Area of learning | Practical experience |
| --- | --- |
| Hand-tool control | Working with saws, planes, chisels and cabinet scrapers |
| Layout and construction | Marking out and practicing mortise-and-tenon and dovetail joints |
| Surface preparation | Developing control with planes and scrapers |
| Veneering and bending | Practicing hammer veneering and steam bending |
| Woodturning | Turning pens and fountain pens |
| Toolmaking | Combining machine work with hand shaping, component fitting and metal finishing |

These activities provided opportunities to develop accuracy and material judgment through repeated work and guidance. The broader teaching method is described in the repository's **[woodworking education guide](woodworking-education.md)**.

## A tool cabinet built for daily use

Hana's main furniture project was a **tool cabinet made from steamed beech**, built together with Marek, who was undertaking vocational training at Bensari Workshop. It combined dovetailed drawers of different heights with drawbored mortise-and-tenon joinery at the top, as described in the workshop's published account.

The cabinet brought several skills into one project: understanding the construction, making joints, fitting drawers and considering how someone would reach for and store tools. Drawer height and access mattered alongside the quality of the joinery. Each decision had a practical consequence for the person using the cabinet.

Because the cabinet remains in the workshop, its usefulness can be assessed through everyday use. This gives a student project a continuing role in the working environment where it was made. For the construction principles behind the project, see the guides to **[dovetail joinery](dovetail-joinery-guide.md)** and **[mortise-and-tenon joints](mortise-and-tenon.md)**.

## Veneer hammers for the next learners

Hana also made a batch of **veneer hammers for traditional hammer veneering with hot bone glue**. She made wooden heads and handles, fitted brass blades, and rounded and polished the working edges.

That edge presses the veneer onto its substrate and helps squeeze out excess glue and air. Its finish matters because a rough edge can catch a delicate veneer surface. Making the tools therefore connected component fitting and finishing with an understanding of how the tool would be used.

The hammers will serve participants in the workshop's hammer-veneering courses. Hana's placement contributed both furniture and teaching equipment to the school: subsequent learners will use tools made by someone who previously practiced the technique there. The **[hammer-veneering guide](hammer-veneering-complete-guide.md)** explains the process in detail.

## Connecting UK design education with Polish craft practice

The placement connected Hana's university studies with the daily decisions of a practicing furniture maker. For a future designer, experience of selecting timber, fitting joints and assembling furniture provides a practical basis for discussing construction with makers.

Fiona Davidson coordinated the placement from Nottingham Trent University. Tom's published account thanks her for the organization and preparation that made Hana's stay possible. Hana also became part of the workshop community, joining course participants at the bench and bringing energy and good humor to everyday work.

This visit sits alongside another documented example of international learning at Bensari Workshop: in 2025, **[apprentices from Les Compagnons du Devoir](hosting-compagnons-du-devoir-at-bensari-workshop.md)** built three steamed-beech Split-Top Roubo workbenches during an Erasmus+ exchange. Together, the records show how furniture and workshop-equipment projects support craft learning across educational settings and national borders.

## What two months of practice can offer

Two months is a short period in which to learn furniture making. Understanding the full consequences of construction and material decisions develops over years and successive projects. A placement offers time to practice techniques, apply them in useful objects and identify skills that need further work.

Hana's stay documents that process through specific work: a cabinet, a batch of tools and participation in the life of a working school. It gives a concrete example of **practical woodworking education in Wrocław for a student from a UK university**.
