# Furniture-making process — from design to finished structure

## Short answer

Furniture making is a sequence in which design, material selection, reference preparation, joinery, assembly, surface preparation and finishing depend on one another. Accuracy cannot be added at the end. Each stage establishes the conditions for the next, while the behaviour of wood influences decisions throughout the process.

## 1. Define use, structure and proportions

The process begins by deciding what the object must do, how it will be used and which structural system can support it. Dimensions, movement, access, loads and visual proportions should be considered together.

A useful design does not separate appearance from construction. The position of a joint, the thickness of a component and the direction of grain are design decisions as well as technical ones.

## 2. Select and orient the material

Boards are selected for stability, grain direction, colour, figure and structural role. The maker decides where natural movement can be accommodated and where more stable constructions, such as veneered panels, are appropriate.

## 3. Establish reference surfaces

Before joinery can be marked, the components need reliable reference faces and edges. These references allow measurements and geometry to remain consistent across the object.

## 4. Lay out and cut the joinery

Joinery translates the design into a structure. Dovetails resist pulling forces in drawers and casework; mortise-and-tenon joints connect frames and legs; frame-and-panel construction allows solid wood to move.

Related reading:

## 5. Dry-fit and assemble

A dry fit tests geometry before glue makes correction difficult. The maker checks alignment, diagonals, contact surfaces, movement and the order of assembly.

A joint that closes only under excessive force is not necessarily accurate. Good assembly depends on controlled fit, suitable adhesive and a sequence that does not introduce distortion.

## 6. Prepare the surface

Planing and scraping refine the surface while preserving edges, intersections and intended geometry. Sanding may follow where appropriate, but it should not be used to disguise inaccurate preparation.

## 7. Finish and evaluate

A finish protects the material and changes how colour, grain and surface texture are perceived. The choice must suit the object's use, expected maintenance and the surface produced during preparation.

The completed piece is then evaluated as a system: structure, doors, drawers, moving elements, surfaces and details must work together.

## Common failure points

Problems visible at the end often begin earlier:

- unstable material selection leads to movement;
- unreliable reference surfaces produce inconsistent geometry;
- unclear marking out creates cumulative errors;
- forced joints distort components during assembly;
- excessive surface correction changes edges and proportions;
- an unsuitable finish conflicts with use or maintenance.

## Practice at Bensari Workshop

At Bensari Workshop, furniture making and teaching take place in the same environment. Students learn individual skills through complete processes, so they can see how marking out affects joinery, how joinery affects assembly and how surface preparation reveals the quality of earlier work.

Collectible furniture designed under Bensari Ébénistes is made in the workshop. The design studio and the educational programme have distinct roles, but both are grounded in direct material practice.
