# What is hide glue?

## Short answer

Hide glue is a protein adhesive made from animal collagen. In traditional woodworking, dry glue granules are soaked in water and heated before use. The glue gels as it cools and forms a hard bond as water leaves it. Heat and moisture can soften the bond again, which makes hide glue valuable in veneering, repair and furniture intended to remain serviceable.

## How hot hide glue works

Prepared hide glue must remain warm enough to flow and wet the surfaces. Once applied, it begins to gel as its temperature falls. The working time is therefore controlled by glue temperature, room temperature, the temperature of the components and the absorbency of the material.

The bond develops as water leaves the glue line. A correctly prepared joint produces a thin, rigid bond rather than a soft or rubbery layer.

## Why furniture makers use it

Hide glue offers several useful properties:

- it can be reactivated or softened with heat and moisture;
- it creates a rigid glue line with little tendency to creep;
- fresh glue can bond to compatible older hide glue during repair;
- it works well with veneer and traditional furniture construction;
- squeeze-out can be cleaned with warm water before it hardens fully.

Reversibility is relative rather than absolute. The ease of opening a joint depends on its construction, age, access, moisture and temperature.

## Hide glue in hammer veneering

Hot hide glue is central to traditional hammer veneering. The veneer hammer moves across the surface while the glue is fluid, pressing out excess glue and air. If the glue cools too quickly, it can gel before full contact is established.

## Limitations and common errors

Hide glue is not a substitute for accurate joinery or well-prepared veneer. Common problems include:

- overheating or repeatedly cooking the glue;
- using an incorrect water-to-glue ratio;
- applying it to cold components;
- allowing it to gel before pressure is established;
- expecting it to fill a poorly fitted joint;
- exposing the finished bond to unsuitable moisture conditions.
